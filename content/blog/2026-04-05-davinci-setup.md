A clean Resolve project saves hours and prevents the kind of late-night mistakes that ruin a grade. Here's the setup we use on every job, from a single reel to a full campaign.

## Start with color management

Before touching a wheel, we set the project to a managed color pipeline so every clip — phone, cinema camera, AI-generated — lands in the same color space. Garbage in, garbage out: managed color is how you make mismatched sources gradeable.

- Set input color space per source, not globally
- Work in a wide timeline space, deliver to Rec.709
- Trust the scopes over your monitor

## A node structure you can actually navigate

We keep every grade on a consistent node tree so anyone on the team can open a project and understand it instantly:

- **Node 1 — Balance:** neutralize, set exposure and white balance
- **Node 2 — Contrast:** shape the curve
- **Node 3 — Creative look:** the signature grade lives here
- **Node 4 — Secondaries:** skin, sky, product isolation
- **Node 5 — Output:** final trim and safety

> Separate correction from creative. When the client asks for "a little warmer," you change one node — not the whole grade.

## LUTs as a starting point, never the finish

We do use LUTs — including our own [Cinematic LUT Pack](store.html) — but always as a *base* on the creative node, dialed back and adjusted to the footage. A LUT applied at full strength on raw footage is how you get that flat, over-baked look.

## Stay organized or pay for it later

Color-code clips, name your nodes, and version your timelines. The discipline feels slow on day one and saves you on delivery day.
